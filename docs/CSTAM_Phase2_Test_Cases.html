CSTAM TuniBot - Test Case Documentation (Phase 2)

Autonomous restaurant delivery robot - ROS 2 Jazzy, Nav2, Gazebo Harmonic. Edge cases for dynamic obstacles (people), low battery, and complex layout / navigation failure recovery.

1. Test setup

  • Environment: Gazebo Harmonic restaurant world (dining room, outdoor terrace through two 0.92 m doorways, robot home corridor with dock and kitchen service hatch). The robot is 0.54 m wide and senses with its own 3D LiDAR plus rear/side range sensors; no ground truth is used for navigation.
  • People: a pedestrian simulator moves 5 people with different behaviours (polite: slows, steps aside, gives way; oblivious: walks at full speed and stops only when physically blocked), at 0.5-1.0 m/s, each scripted to create a specific encounter on the robot's routes. Extra scenarios place a guest standing in one or both terrace doors.
  • Battery: 10 Wh Li-ion model integrated on simulated time from real odometry (4 W idle load plus motion power); charging (60 W, tapering above 80%) only while stationary within 0.5 m of the dock.
  • How results are measured: each acceptance run launches the full headless stack, passes a 23-check startup health test, sends delivery requests and records a 2 Hz trace (robot ground truth, every person, delivery state, collision-monitor action). The pedestrian simulator reports contacts and clearances from ground truth, and attributes each contact to the robot or the person (the robot is not at fault if it was stationary or creeping while the person walked into it).
  • Automated tests: 66 unit tests (colcon test --packages-select cstam_phase1), all passing: battery model 6, delivery control and recovery 20, pedestrian simulator 18, people tracking 12, locations and footprint clearance 4, geometry 3, others 3.

2. Dynamic obstacles (people)

People are detected only with the robot's own sensors: points not explained by the static map are clustered and tracked with a constant-velocity Kalman multi-tracker. Each track is written into the costmaps with a 2 s predicted path, and a person-only collision monitor stops or slows the robot (stop zone in the direction of travel; 0.07 m/s cap with a person within 1.4 m ahead; speed scaled so the footprint cannot reach a person within 1.5 s).

4. Complex layout and navigation failure recovery

Terrace deliveries go through 0.92 m doorways (robot 0.54 m wide). A trip between zones is routed square through a door via inside/outside staging poses. Before driving through, the robot asks the planner for the path through the door; a path much longer than the door (> 2.5 m) means the door is blocked.

5. Known limits

  • Charging is simulated by proximity to the dock pose, not by charger contact.
  • In the demo, the battery level is set to 19% mid-delivery and charging is fast-forwarded to 80%, both by parameter (shown in the video captions); the abort, re-queue, docking and resume logic run unmodified.
  • People are simulated with scripted routes; behaviour of real crowds will be more varied.
  • The 1.0 m/s configuration was verified in the demo recordings; the multi-run statistics above were collected at 0.15-0.45 m/s.
